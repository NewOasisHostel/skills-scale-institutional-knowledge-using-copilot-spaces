# OctoAcme Project Management Process Overview

OctoAcme employs a structured and collaborative project management approach designed to align stakeholders, enable iterative delivery, and ensure product quality. The process begins with Project Initiation, where new ideas or feature proposals are validated through a lightweight planning phase. Key deliverables at this stage include a Project One-pager, stakeholder alignment, a high-level timeline, and an initial risk register. Once approved, the initiative is formally planned—work is broken down into manageable increments, dependencies and risks are identified, and a prioritized backlog with acceptance criteria is developed. Planning artifacts such as release plans, milestone maps, and a documented Definition of Done guide the team through execution.

Roles and responsibilities are clearly defined to facilitate smooth delivery. The Project Manager (PM) is accountable for coordination, scheduling, risk management, and ensuring effective communication across the team. The Product Manager (PdM) owns the product vision, prioritizes work, and measures outcomes, while Developers implement features, maintain tests and documentation, and contribute to design and planning. QA/Testing assures acceptance criteria and product quality, and Stakeholders provide input and approvals. These personas engage in activities ranging from sprint planning and daily standups to retrospectives and incident reviews, ensuring that responsibilities are clearly allocated throughout the project lifecycle.

Communication is a cornerstone of OctoAcme’s methodology. The team follows a regular cadence: weekly syncs between PM and PdM, twice-weekly standups for the delivery team, and monthly updates to stakeholders. Communication templates are standardized for status reporting, incidents, and risk visibility. Escalation paths are well-defined, ensuring that blockers and critical issues promptly reach the appropriate decision-makers. Project boards and shared documentation serve as the source of truth, and risks are proactively tracked, monitored, and communicated throughout the project's duration.

Quality assurance is deeply embedded in the execution and release processes. Automated and manual testing are required for all significant changes, including unit, integration, and end-to-end smoke tests. CI pipelines enforce code quality, linting, and security scanning before reviews. Thorough pre-release checklists, standardized deployment guidelines, and incident playbooks minimize release risk and improve observability. Continuous improvement is achieved through regular retrospectives, actionable follow-ups, and measuring the impact of implemented changes—reinforcing a culture where lessons learned are rapidly translated into process enhancements.
